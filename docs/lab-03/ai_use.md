## Reflection
Working on Lab 3 helped me move past simple CRUD operations and understand how a real multi-role system fits together in practice. By dividing the system between Requester, IT Staff, and Admin, I learned how each role has strict operational limits—like keeping internal notes hidden from requesters, letting staff adjust internal priority without modifying the requester's requested priority, and preventing requesters from changing ticket status directly. A major eye-opener was learning about security vulnerabilities, such as returning 404 instead of 403 on cross-user tickets to prevent ID enumeration, and locking new accounts into a forced password change on first login. I also had to think through critical edge cases, like writing safety guards so admins cannot deactivate themselves or lock out the last remaining admin, and requiring resolution notes before closing tickets. While using the AI sped up writing boilerplate and UI markup, I quickly learned that I could not just blindly accept its output; I had to catch business rule mismatches myself and fix flaky Playwright tests by replacing guesswork timers with real network response checks. Overall, this project taught me how to think through authorization boundaries, error handling, and system defensibility like a real software developer.
